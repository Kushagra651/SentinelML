Question answered: "How do you know your drift alerts are trustworthy?"

Setup [MEASURED]: reference = train split (30,162 rows). Clean windows
sampled from the TEST split (independent). 14 features per check.
200 windows per false-alarm cell; 50 repeats per detection cell.
Window sizes 30, 100, 500, 1000. Drift intensities 0.0 to 0.6.
Full run took about 11 minutes.

D0. Current settings [CODE]
KS p-threshold 0.05; chi-square p-threshold 0.05; PSI warning 0.1,
critical 0.2; PSI bins 10; minimum samples 30.
Old rule: overall_drifted = ANY of 14 features has p < 0.05.

D1. False positive rate (alarms on CLEAN windows)

D2. Theory check
1 - 0.95^14 = 0.512  [COMPUTED]
SIMPLE DEFENSE: "If I run 14 tests at 5% each, the chance at least one
fires by luck is about 51%. My measured 44-62% matches that. So the old
rule was not 'broken code'; it was a statistics problem called multiple
comparisons."
LIMIT: Features are not perfectly independent, so theory is approximate.

D3. Windows sampled from the reference itself (biased check)
Raw rule: 0.52 / 0.515 / 0.415 / 0.385 for W = 30/100/500/1000.
WHAT: When the clean window comes from the same data the reference was
built from, there is slight bias at W=1000 (0.385 vs 0.44).
LIMIT: With 200 windows, the error bar is about +-0.07, so at W=30 and
W=100 the difference is just noise.

D4. Detection rate by drift size (key rows)

 100    | Bonferroni           | 0.24      | 0.24 | 0.90 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00

(Note: FPR here is 0.06/0.08/0.42 vs D1's 0.065/0.115/0.44. Different
random batches of windows, 50 vs 200 repeats. Quote D1 for FPR.)

D5. Final recommended rule: Bonferroni AND PSI>=0.1 (per feature)
 window | FPR (clean) | detect 0.1 | detect 0.2 | detect 0.3
 1000   | 0.015       | 0.00       | 0.00       | 1.00
 500    | 0.050       | 0.02       | 0.02       | 1.00
 100    | 0.140       | 0.24       | 0.26       | 0.44
 30     | 0.280       | 0.44       | 0.44       | 0.48
SIMPLE DEFENSE: "At 1000 samples this rule almost never false-alarms
(1.5%), ignores harmless drift (0% at intensity 0.1 and 0.2), and always
catches the moderate drift that costs 1.2 points of accuracy (100% at
0.3)."
LIMIT: The harmless/moderate line (between 0.2 and 0.3) is measured on
synthetic covariate drift only. Below 100 samples the rule is unusable.

D6. Why Bonferroni
Bonferroni divides 0.05 by the number of tests (14), so each feature
needs p < about 0.0036. BH (Benjamini-Hochberg) is a softer version.
They gave nearly equal results (0.065 vs 0.070 at W=1000).
SIMPLE DEFENSE: "If you buy 14 lottery tickets, you make each ticket
harder to win so the total stays at 5%."

D7. Why add PSI>=0.1 on top
Significance (p-value) says "the change is real". PSI says "the change
is big". With many samples, tiny harmless shifts become 'significant'.
At W>=500 even intensity 0.1 (harmless, -0.18 pp) was detected 80-100%
of the time with p-values alone.
SIMPLE DEFENSE: "I need both: real AND big enough to matter."
Note: PSI>=0.1 alone fires on 87-100% of clean windows, but combined
per feature with Bonferroni it works. (Cause of the PSI-alone problem is
a hypothesis, not verified.)

D8. The old 'critical' rule has a cliff
p<0.05 AND PSI>=0.2 at W=1000: detect 0.3 is only 0.02, then 1.00 at
0.45. SIMPLE DEFENSE: "It would have missed the moderate drift that
costs 1.2 points. That is why I picked PSI>=0.1 instead of 0.2."

D9. Detection delay (median samples until the rule fires)
- Raw rule: 30-40 samples at every intensity.
- Bonferroni/BH: 300 samples at intensity 0.1; 30-50 at 0.2 and above.
- Combined rule at intensity 0.3: about 100 samples.
- Old critical rule: detected only 85% of intensity-0.1 runs and 95% at
  0.2 (delay table).
SIMPLE DEFENSE: "My new rule reacts in about 100 samples instead of 30.
That is the price of fewer false alarms."

D10. Traffic needed for each window in a 6 h check [COMPUTED]
 30 samples  -> 5 requests/hour
 100         -> 16.7 per hour
 500         -> 83.3 per hour
 1000        -> 166.7 per hour
SIMPLE DEFENSE: "To use window 1000 with a 6 hour check, I need about
167 requests per hour."
LIMIT: Real traffic is zero (section I). So the 6 h interval is NOT
justified by data. Honest claim: window and interval are configurable,
and I know the minimum traffic needed. Detector flags stay OFF.

D11. Worst-case time from drift start to retrain start
6 h (next drift check) + 0.5 h (retrain trigger offset) = 6.5 h
[CODE/COMPUTED], plus training time (~5 s for this model, plus DAG
overhead).

D12. Features that false-alarm most on clean windows
native_country 21.5% at W=30; marital_status and occupation also high.
LIMIT: Hypothesis only: chi-square is unreliable when many categories
are rare (expected counts near 0). Not verified.

D13. Pick of window
Script printed "window=500" only because it sorts by smaller window.
W=500 FPR is exactly 0.05 (upper error bar about 0.09). So W=1000 is the
safer pick.

D14. Unit tests for the detector change: 5 tests passed (none,
Bonferroni, BH, PSI gate, empty list). Flags: DRIFT_CORRECTION,
DRIFT_MIN_PSI, DRIFT_MIN_SAMPLES; all default OFF (none, 0, 30).

SIMPLE DEFENSE: "The numbers match the queue formula, so the slow
latency is waiting time, not slow math."

SIMPLE DEFENSE: "One prediction takes about 20 ms of work."

harsher than 100 real people. Client and server share one Windows

=====================================================================
M. HARD QUESTIONS, SHORT ANSWERS
=====================================================================
Q: Why did you build this?
A: Models degrade silently when inputs shift and labels arrive late. I
built a watcher plus guard-railed retraining, then measured whether it
was worth it: severe drift costs 6.7 points, retraining wins back ~80%.

Q: Why those metrics?
A: ROC-AUC for promotion (no cut-off, handles 75/25 split), PR-AUC for
the rare class, accuracy against the 75.43% baseline, Brier/ECE for
calibration. Not accuracy alone.

Q: Is 86.67% good?
A: It beats always guessing (75.43%) and a fair logistic regression
(84.18%), with a paired CI that excludes zero.

Q: What went wrong and what did you fix?
A: My detector false-alarmed on 44% of clean windows (14 tests at 5%).
Fixed with Bonferroni + PSI>=0.1: 1.5%. My accuracy gate sat below the
dumb baseline; raised to 0.76.

Q: Is your detector deployed?
A: Code is in, flags are OFF, because it needs about 167 requests/hour
and I have no real traffic. I will not pretend otherwise.

Q: What are the biggest weaknesses?
A: Synthetic drift, one dataset, one fixed split, test set reused for
gates, no real traffic, single worker, agent not evaluated.

Q: Why not MLflow Model Registry?
A: Two sources of truth. The API hot-reloads from one JSON file; MLflow
only records history, linked by version_tag.

Q: Where is the bottleneck?
A: One uvicorn worker: ~50 req/s, ~20 ms of work per request; the rest
is queueing.
